Abstract

Long-horizon vision-language navigation requires knowing not only where a robot is, but whether its progress remains consistent with the instruction. Rabbit-RobotNav investigates historical semantic-spatial evidence for deviation diagnosis and recovery-anchor selection without accessing evaluation labels. The framework separates recorded observations from trusted memory and checks instruction, history, and transition consistency. Controlled MP3D cases provide preliminary diagnostic evidence; real-robot experiments expose unstable object identity and insufficient heading fusion during room mapping. Reliable mobile mapping, navigation gains, and autonomous recovery remain unverified.

Method Overview

  1. Record before trusting. Preserve time-stamped observations, poses, and source records. Repeated detections do not establish physical identity or a reliable landmark.
  2. Diagnose with sufficient evidence. Compare instruction, history, and spatial transitions. The read-only adapter explicitly reports insufficient evidence instead of treating a missing signal as normal navigation.
  3. Recover from a supported state. Investigate an earlier trustworthy state as an anchor. Real-robot anchor validation and recovery execution are still open.

From Observations to Reliable History

Archived real-robot sessions now replay into 1,149 raw states and 3,477 semantic observations. These are repeated observations, not unique objects. None has yet been admitted as a trusted state or landmark.

Read-only diagnosis. All 1,149 replayed states lack sufficient consistency evidence; no reliable recovery anchor is available. Zero supported deviation detections here means not evaluable, not successful navigation. View the diagnostic audit.

Real-Robot Room Survey

A human-controlled survey now provides an actual occupancy grid and synchronized sensor records: approximately 229 seconds, 2,747 laser scans, and 225 RGB keyframes. Collection is complete; room-map acceptance is not.

Map review pending. Radial streaks and inconsistent return estimates prevent a reliable geometry claim. Object detection was not run, and no metric landmark has been verified. This separate survey does not extend the 1,149-state historical replay; all robot movement was human-controlled.

Why heading evidence matters

The recorded fused heading stayed at zero despite turns in raw odometry. An isolated A/B test of the actual extended Kalman filter (EKF) enabled only odometry angular-velocity fusion. The candidate still failed: heading changed by −0.077 rad, versus −7.122 rad in the recorded input.

Supported finding, not a completed fix. The driver reports angular-velocity variance of 1,000 whenever any velocity component is nonzero; the source, executable constants, and recorded messages agree. This explains strong down-weighting in the tested candidate, not the sole cause of every map artifact. Source audit.

Latest IMU check. A separate stationary recording received 500 raw inertial measurement unit (IMU) messages at approximately 20 Hz. The filtered IMU topic selected by the EKF had no publisher. Static screening is complete, but turning response and measurement reliability remain unverified; no fusion candidate has been selected. Stationary evidence & original plots.

Next: record synchronized IMU and odometry during a separately authorized, controlled turn with an independent angle reference, before selecting another fusion candidate or accepting the room map.
