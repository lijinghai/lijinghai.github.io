Rabbit-RobotNav: History-Aware Error Diagnosis

MASTER'S THESIS PROJECT · WORK IN PROGRESS

Thesis Experiments Code

Abstract

Long-horizon vision-language navigation requires knowing not only where a robot is, but whether its progress remains consistent with the instruction. Rabbit-RobotNav investigates historical semantic-spatial evidence for deviation diagnosis and recovery-anchor selection without accessing evaluation labels. The framework separates recorded observations from trusted memory and checks instruction, history, and transition consistency. Controlled MP3D cases provide preliminary diagnostic evidence; real-robot replay exposes insufficient evidence and unstable object identity. Reliable mobile mapping, navigation gains, and autonomous recovery remain unverified.

Method Overview

  1. Record before trusting. Preserve time-stamped observations, poses, and source records. Repeated detections do not establish physical identity or a reliable landmark.
  2. Diagnose with sufficient evidence. Compare instruction, history, and spatial transitions. The read-only adapter explicitly reports insufficient evidence instead of treating a missing signal as normal navigation.
  3. Recover from a supported state. Investigate an earlier trustworthy state as an anchor. Real-robot anchor validation and recovery execution are still open.

From Observations to Reliable History

Archived real-robot sessions now replay into 1,149 raw states and 3,477 semantic observations. These are repeated observations, not unique objects. None has yet been admitted as a trusted state or landmark.

Read-only diagnosis. All 1,149 replayed states lack sufficient consistency evidence; no reliable recovery anchor is available. Zero supported deviation detections here means not evaluable, not successful navigation. View the diagnostic audit.

Toward a Verified Room Map

A bounded stationary preflight recorded 179 laser scans over 15 seconds (approximately 12.04 Hz), alongside three saved RGB frames.

Only 31.72% of laser beams were valid. No occupancy grid or map-frame transform was obtained; room coverage and landmark accuracy cannot yet be evaluated.

Next: a human-supervised room survey, followed by independent checks of room geometry, object identity, and landmark position.
